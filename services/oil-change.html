Choose Your Oil

The Right Oil for Your Vehicle

Not all engines are the same. We help you choose the correct oil type and viscosity for your make, model, mileage, and driving conditions.

Conventional

Standard mineral-based oil for older vehicles with simple engine designs and lower mileage. Reliable protection at an affordable price point.

  • Recommended for vehicles under 75,000 miles
  • Change interval: every 3,000–5,000 miles
  • Best for older model years pre-2010
Most Popular

Full Synthetic

Engineered for maximum performance and protection. Required by most modern turbocharged and high-performance engines. Superior thermal stability and flow at low temperatures.

  • Change interval: every 5,000–7,500 miles
  • Required for most 2015+ vehicles
  • Better fuel economy & engine longevity

High-Mileage

Specially formulated for vehicles with over 75,000 miles. Contains seal conditioners to reduce oil consumption and minimize leaks from aging engine seals.

  • For vehicles over 75,000 miles
  • Reduces oil consumption & seepage
  • Available in conventional or synthetic blend

Why It Matters

Regular Oil Changes Protect Your Engine

Engine oil lubricates hundreds of moving parts, dissipates heat, and carries contaminants to the filter. As oil ages, it breaks down and loses its viscosity — allowing metal-to-metal contact that causes expensive internal wear.

Skipping oil changes is the single most common cause of premature engine failure. A $50–$100 oil change protects an engine worth $5,000–$15,000 or more. At AutoTrek in Littleton, CO, we make it fast and affordable to stay on schedule.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Oil Needs Changing

Oil change or oil pressure warning light — don't ignore this. Stop driving and check oil level immediately.

Dark, gritty, or thick oil on dipstick — oil has exceeded its service life and is carrying contaminants.

Loud ticking or knocking from the engine — insufficient lubrication causing metal components to contact.

Burning oil smell inside the cabin — oil may be leaking onto hot engine components.

Exceeded mileage interval — you're past 3,000–7,500 miles since your last change (depending on oil type).
